## Dataset

The transaction data has one row per order and includes the following fields:

| Column | Description |
|---|---|
| `sku_number` | Product identifier |
| `inventory_type` | Inventory classification |
| `stocking_type` | How the item is stocked (e.g., MTS = Make-to-Stock, MTO = Make-to-Order) |
| `lead_time` | Replenishment lead time in days |
| `unit_price` | Price per unit |
| `manufacturing_site` | Site code (prefix indicates country, e.g., `PL-`, `CA-`, `JP-`, `US-`) |
| `division_code` | Division identifier |
| `transaction_date` | Date of the transaction |
| `order_quantity` | Units ordered |

## Part 1 — Assignment 1: Data Cleaning & Inspection

**Goal:** Standardize, inspect, and clean the raw transaction data so it is reliable for downstream inventory analysis.

**What was done**

- **Column names:** Renamed columns to follow the same naming convention as the metadata file.
- **Anomaly detection (`describe()`):**
  - *Lead time:* Typical values are 14, 21, and 28 days, but the maximum was 2,800 days. These outliers looked like values multiplied by 100, so they were divided by 100 to match the rest of the data.
  - *Unit price:* 10 negative prices were found, mostly on transactions with positive quantities, which suggests data-entry errors. They were converted to absolute values. These should be verified in case any are legitimate refunds or adjustments.
  - *Order quantity:* 40,630 negative quantities were found. These may be returns, cancellations, or inventory adjustments, so they were **kept unchanged** rather than altering potentially valid records.
- **Missing values:**

  | Column | Missing | Treatment |
  |---|---|---|
  | `sku_number` | 5,238 | Rows dropped (a transaction with no SKU can't be tied to a product) |
  | `inventory_type` | 19,443 | Filled with `"Unknown"` |
  | `stocking_type` | 9,900 | Filled with `"Unknown"` |
  | `manufacturing_site` | 153,569 | Filled with `"Unknown"` (~20% of rows, too many to drop) |
  | `division_code` | 55,571 | Filled with `"Unknown"` |

  After cleaning, no column had missing values. Filling with `"Unknown"` (rather than guessing) preserves the transactions without assigning incorrect classifications.
- **Dimensional checks:** Counted unique SKUs, manufacturing sites, and divisions; listed the top and bottom 10 transactions by order quantity; and created a `total_sales` column (`order_quantity × unit_price`) to find the top and bottom 10 transactions by sales value.

**Key findings from the cleaned dataset**

- 612 unique SKUs
- 19 known manufacturing sites (plus "Unknown")
- 54 known divisions (plus "Unknown")

## Part 2 — Assignment 2: Safety Stock Analysis

**Goal:** Calculate safety stock for MTS SKUs, since these items are held in inventory and need a buffer against demand uncertainty.

**Approach**

1. Loaded the cleaned dataset from Part 1 (`prepared_data.csv`).
2. Filtered to Make-to-Stock transactions (`stocking_type == "MTS"`).
3. Grouped by SKU and calculated the minimum, maximum, mean, median, variance, and standard deviation of order quantity, plus average lead time.
4. Calculated Z-scores with `scipy.stats.norm.ppf()` for three service levels:

   | Service level | Z-score |
   |---|---|
   | 75% | 0.6745 |
   | 90% | 1.2816 |
   | 95% | 1.6449 |

5. Calculated safety stock per SKU with:

   ```
   Safety Stock = Z × σ(order quantity) × √(average lead time)
   ```

**Results at the 95% service level** (as produced by the notebook's current code)

| Metric | SKU | Safety stock (units) |
|---|---|---|
| Highest | `53767219` | 372.58 |
| Lowest | `9BC2A5C4` | 52.37 |
| Average across all SKUs | — | 141.61 |

The transformed SKU table contains 482 SKUs with no missing values. Safety stock varies widely across SKUs, driven by differences in demand variability and lead time.

## Notes and Limitations

- Negative order quantities were retained because their meaning (returns vs. errors) can't be confirmed from the available data.
- Records with `"Unknown"` manufacturing sites have no country attribution.
- The corrected negative unit prices should be verified against the source system.
- Safety stock here uses the standard-deviation-of-demand × √lead-time formula with average lead time per SKU; it assumes lead time is constant and demand is independent across periods.
